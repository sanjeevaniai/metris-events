AI is already in your organization. What can you actually prove about it?

Adoption moved faster than anyone could plan for. Licences were bought, pilots went live, employees found their own uses, and AI entered workflows, decisions and customer conversations. The question has changed from whether to use AI to what it is actually changing inside the business, and what evidence you have that it is working.

Most organizations can describe the activity in detail. They know which tools were purchased, how many people have access, how many pilots launched, how often the application is used and which policies were approved. Every one of those numbers can be accurate while the questions that matter stay unanswered. What changed. Whether the change created value. Whether the controls that exist on paper are operating. Whether the people responsible for supervising AI can actually do it.

These sessions look at that gap from three seats. What you own determines what you can see, so the conversation is different depending on where you sit.

The objective is not to turn executives into AI specialists. It is to make them considerably harder to fool with AI metrics.
